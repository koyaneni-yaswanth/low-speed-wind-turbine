# United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) Alignment

The PAWAN SHAKTI engineering research initiative explores technological pathways toward decentralized, low-carbon power generation. The project aligns with three specific United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), focusing on practical engineering contributions rather than claiming instantaneous global solutions.

---

## SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

> **Target 7.1 & 7.2**: Ensure universal access to affordable, reliable, and modern energy services; increase substantially the share of renewable energy in the global energy mix.

### How PAWAN SHAKTI Aligns & Contributes:
- **Expanding Renewable Harvesting Boundaries**: Over $70\%$ of the world's populated landmass experiences average surface wind velocities below $5.0\text{ m/s}$. Conventional small-wind technologies operate effectively only in coastal or high-elevation corridors, leaving inland populations reliant exclusively on fossil generators or intermittent solar. Investigating micro-turbines with cut-in speeds between $2.0\text{ and }2.5\text{ m/s}$ **supports exploration of** viable renewable generation across inland rural communities.
- **Solar-Wind Complementarity**: Solar PV is inherently constrained to daylight hours. By exploring micro-wind energy harvesting that functions during evening and nighttime convective breeze periods, PAWAN SHAKTI **contributes toward** reducing the battery storage capacity required for continuous off-grid rural power systems.
- **Affordable Modular Hardware**: Investigating low-cost materials (thermopolymers, commercial modular extrusions, and accessible manufacturing methods) **aims to lower** the capital cost barrier for decentralized energy hardware.

---

## SDG 9: Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure

> **Target 9.4 & 9.5**: Upgrade infrastructure and retrofit industries to make them sustainable; enhance scientific research and upgrade technological capabilities.

### How PAWAN SHAKTI Aligns & Contributes:
- **Decentralized Infrastructure Resilience**: Powering distributed micro-infrastructure—such as remote environmental monitoring stations, rural telecommunication relays, and agricultural telemetry—frequently relies on expensive grid extensions or diesel generators. PAWAN SHAKTI **investigates compact renewable infrastructure** capable of operating as an auxiliary power unit in low-wind conditions.
- **Academic & Applied Engineering Research**: Conducted within the research ecosystem of VNIT Nagpur and supported by ANRF grant frameworks and the VNIT Ventures Vault Foundation (V3F), the project **supports student and institutional R&D capacity** in mechanical design, additive manufacturing, and renewable power systems.
- **Local Manufacturability**: By utilizing standardized components, desktop 3D printing, and modular chassis framing, the system **explores decentralized production models** where small-scale hardware can be assembled and maintained by local technicians without specialized overseas supply chains.

---

## SDG 13: Climate Action

> **Target 13.2**: Integrate climate change measures into national policies, strategies, and planning.

### How PAWAN SHAKTI Aligns & Contributes:
- **Displacement of Fossil Fuel Backup**: In off-grid and weak-grid environments, nighttime power shortages are overwhelmingly met with small diesel and kerosene generators, which emit significant carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$), and unburned particulate matter. A functional 100 W micro-wind system operating at modest capacity factor **contributes to offsetting** fuel consumption in low-power baseline applications.
- **Low-Carbon Lifecycle Investigation**: By evaluating recyclable polymers (ABS/PETG), mechanical longevity through magnetic gear couplings, and modular component replacement, the research **supports exploration of** sustainable lifecycle engineering for distributed renewable hardware.

---

## Evidentiary Boundaries & Honest Framing

To maintain academic and professional integrity:
- PAWAN SHAKTI **does not claim** to have eliminated energy poverty or replaced utility power grids.
- The project **is an early-stage prototype and experimental research program** evaluating the technical limits and trade-offs of micro-wind harvesting in low-velocity wind regimes.
- Its ultimate societal impact depends entirely upon completing structured multi-season field validation, proving mechanical durability, and establishing repeatable manufacturing economics.
