# Engineering Challenges & Practical Lessons Learned

Authentic engineering research is characterized by confronting physical constraints, debugging mechanical failures, and systematically refining designs. This document details the genuine technical challenges encountered during the design, fabrication, and testing of the PAWAN SHAKTI prototypes.

---

## Challenge 01: Low Aerodynamic Starting Torque vs. Transmission Stiction

```
[ Problem ]
   The initial prototype failed to begin rotating in wind speeds below 3.5 m/s, despite CFD
   calculations indicating positive aerodynamic lift at 2.0 m/s.
      |
      v
[ Investigation ]
   Static torque measurement revealed that the combined breakaway torque of the 1:10 planetary
   gearbox, generator cogging, and deep-groove rubber seals exceeded the static aerodynamic
   torque generated by stationary blades.
      |
      v
[ Modification ]
   1. Transitioned rotor blade profile to the ultra-high-camber Selig S1223 airfoil.
   2. Increased blade solidity (multi-blade impellers).
   3. Removed heavy grease from prototype bearings, replacing it with low-viscosity synthetic lubricant.
      |
      v
[ Result ]
   Spontaneous aerodynamic cut-in wind speed dropped from > 3.5 m/s down to 2.3–2.5 m/s on the test bench.
      |
      v
[ Key Learning ]
   Static breakaway torque (stiction) is the single greatest bottleneck for low-wind micro-turbines.
   Aerodynamic design must optimize static starting torque coefficient (C_Ts), not merely peak dynamic power coefficient (C_p).
      |
      v
[ Next Engineering Step ]
   Implement low-cogging coreless ironless axial-flux generators and evaluate magnetic contactless gearing.
```

## Challenge 02: Additive Manufacturing (FDM) Tolerances & Gear Mesh Friction

```
[ Problem ]
   3D-printed ABS planetary and spur gear teeth exhibited noticeable mechanical binding, high
   frictional heating, and acoustic rattling at rotational speeds exceeding 300 RPM.
      |
      v
[ Investigation ]
   FDM 3D printing introduces surface roughness (layer stairstepping Ra ~ 15–20 µm) and localized
   dimensional expansion (+0.15 mm tooth thickness error), causing zero-backlash tight meshing
   and pitch-line binding.
      |
      v
[ Modification ]
   1. Redesigned tooth profiles in Fusion 360 with deliberate 0.25 mm tooth flank backlash clearance.
   2. Switched from standard module 1.5 to module 2.5 spur teeth for greater tooth bending strength.
   3. Tested post-processing vapor smoothing on ABS components and applied PTFE dry-film lubricant.
      |
      v
[ Result ]
   Gearbox mechanical transmission efficiency improved significantly; binding was eliminated up to 600 RPM.
      |
      v
[ Key Learning ]
   Standard nominal gear equations cannot be 3D printed directly without explicit backlash offsets
   tailored to printer nozzle extrusion physics.
      |
      v
[ Next Engineering Step ]
   Transition critical high-speed planetary stages from 3D-printed ABS to CNC-machined POM (Delrin)
   or precision nylon-injection molded gears.
```

## Challenge 03: Multi-Rotor Friction Compounding in Mark-5 (KECS)

```
[ Problem ]
   The 5-rotor Mark-5 prototype ("Project KECS") captured substantial total frontal area, but
   demanded significantly higher wind velocity to start spinning than the 3-rotor Mark-3 unit.
      |
      v
[ Investigation ]
   Mechanically linking five separate rotor pinions to a single central transmission gear creates
   five simultaneous friction nodes. Any minute angular misalignment across the aluminum frame
   caused uneven tooth loading and multiplied total parasitic friction.
      |
      v
[ Modification ]
   Conducted comparative benchmark tests between Configuration A (3-rotor), Configuration B (4-rotor),
   and Configuration C (5-rotor) as documented in the testing protocols.
      |
      v
[ Result ]
   Configuration B (4-rotor) provided the optimal empirical balance between captured frontal area
   and mechanical transmission complexity. Configuration C suffered from compounding mechanical drag.
      |
      v
[ Key Learning ]
   Simply adding more rotors to a common mechanical gear train has diminishing returns due to
   frictional compounding. Mechanical simplification is essential for low-power reliability.
      |
      v
[ Next Engineering Step ]
   Investigate independent generator per rotor versus centralized magnetic-gear power combining.
```

## Challenge 04: DC Bus Voltage Sizing (The 240V Trap)

```
[ Problem ]
   Early electrical architecture calculations explored scaling a commercial DFIG topology to 100 W,
   which resulted in an unusually high DC link voltage target (~240 V).
      |
      v
[ Investigation ]
   At 100 W output, a 240 V bus reduces rated current to just 0.356 A. Calculating the required
   LSC filter inductor via standard equations yielded an impractically massive value of 127 mH,
   along with severe converter switching inefficiencies at low power.
      |
      v
[ Modification ]
   Re-evaluated complete power electronics calculations for low-voltage direct current (LVDC) standards.
      |
      v
[ Result ]
   Selected a nominal 24 V DC system bus (operating current 3.2–4.2 A). Filter inductance dropped
   to standard commercial levels (< 2.5 mH) with standard compact passive components.
      |
      v
[ Key Learning ]
   High-voltage utility-scale wind turbine topologies (DFIG / 690V) cannot be linearly scaled down
   to micro-scale systems without encountering severe passive component scaling penalties.
      |
      v
[ Next Engineering Step ]
   Design a dedicated 24V synchronous buck-boost MPPT charge controller tailored for the 100W DC generator.
```

## Challenge 05: Field Wind Measurement & Dynamic Logging Gaps

```
[ Problem ]
   Early bench tests logged electrical voltage and current, but lacked synchronized, instantaneous
   wind speed measurements, making it impossible to determine true aerodynamic efficiency.
      |
      v
[ Investigation ]
   Natural outdoor wind fluctuates second-by-second. Comparing tests recorded on different days
   without simultaneous wind speed and rotor RPM logging produced inconsistent, unrepeatable data.
      |
      v
[ Modification ]
   Authored the comprehensive Intern Task Brief and implemented an ESP32 multi-sensor logging gateway
   integrating a cup anemometer on an interrupt pin, optical RPM sensing, and voltage/current ADCs.
      |
      v
[ Result ]
   Enabled simultaneous, timestamped data logging across all configurations into a unified CSV format,
   creating the empirical dataset necessary to train a baseline ML regression model.
      |
      v
[ Key Learning ]
   Wind turbine performance data is meaningless without simultaneous, high-frequency atmospheric wind logging.
      |
      v
[ Next Engineering Step ]
   Calibrate ESP32 datalogger against laboratory pitot-static reference tubes in the VNIT wind tunnel.
```
